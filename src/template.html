Part names in 30 seconds

Every part has a letter plus a number. The letter says what it is. The number tells you the channel.

LetterPartLooks like
RResistorTiny black block (SMD) or a striped bead
CCapacitorRound can with a stripe on the minus side
UChip (op-amp)Black rectangle with legs on two sides
QTransistorSmall 3-leg part
JJack or connectorThe sockets you plug cables into
ETest point or link pinSmall square pad or pin
SWSwitchPush buttons
The number trick. The channel number comes first, then two fixed digits. R162 is resistor 62 on channel 1. On channel 7 the same part is R762. On channel 18 it’s R1862. The app fills these in for you.
Schematic symbols you’ll meet
Schematic vs board picture

A schematic is a map of connections. Parts are drawn as symbols and placed wherever reads clearly, not where they sit on the board.

A board layout shows where parts physically sit. Use the schematic to understand why, and the layout or your meter to find where.

When in doubt, your meter’s beep mode is the truth: if two points beep, they’re connected.
